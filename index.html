Summary of Malaysia Weather Data - 2022

A comparative analysis of key meteorological trends across states.

🏭Air Pollution Level Detected by Station
The Air Pollution Level map visualizes data from various monitoring stations. Each circle represents a station, and the size indicates the air pollution level (API) recorded in 2022. Areas with larger circles, such as specific spots in Selangor and Penang, signify higher pollution levels that warrant closer attention and policy review.
A key observation is the spatial overview, which shows that hotspots tend to cluster near industrial centers on the West Coast of Peninsular Malaysia. States like Sabah and Sarawak show generally lower and more dispersed pollution levels, suggesting a correlation between economic activity and localized air quality issues.
🌡️Temperature
The graph clearly demonstrates the stable, tropical climate of Malaysia, with the average temperature across all states remain consistent throughout the year. The tight clustering of the lines indicates minimal national temperature variation, the overall thermal experience is very uniform.
A noticeable trend is the general temperature starts to decline during the mid-year months (May to September). The red dashed line specifically marks the Southwest Monsoon transition period (March onward), a seasonal wind pattern that brings air from the ocean to land, resulting in decreasing in temperature.
🌫️Visibility
This heatmap visualisation illustrates the average monthly visibility (in kilometres) across various Malaysian states throughout the year by color luminance. This design allows for easy comparison of visibility trends between states and over time, highlighting both seasonal and regional variations.
Most states generally experience high visibility throughout the year, but certain months and regions show reduced visibility possibly due to factors such as haze, rainfall, or monsoon seasons. Pulau Pinang show noticeable fluctuations, suggesting seasonal weather patterns affecting air clarity.
The radial chart illustrates Malaysia's average humidity levels by month for the selected state. The deep blue shades showing that the state generally experiences high moisture. Humidity tends to peak during the early and late months of the year, coinciding with the monsoon seasons.
The bar chart compares mean wind speeds across Malaysian states for January. Kelantan records the highest wind speed, suggesting stronger monsoon influence in the east coast region. In contrast, states like Selangor and Labuan experience calmer winds, reflecting regional climatic variations across Malaysia.
