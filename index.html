Summary of Malaysia Weather Data in Year 2022

Mean Air Pollution Across Year

This map visualizes the mean air pollution levels across various monitoring stations in Malaysia for the year 2022. Each circle represents a station, with the size constant and the color indicating the pollution level based on the recorded values. Darker red shades correspond to higher pollution levels, allowing for quick identification of areas with elevated air quality concerns. Users can hover over each station to see the exact location and pollution value, providing both a spatial overview and detailed data insights. This visualization helps policymakers and the public understand regional variations in air quality and identify hotspots that may require attention.

Average temperature by Month and State

The chart above presents the average temperature by month and state across Malaysia. Each colored line represents a different state, allowing for comparison of temperature trends throughout the year. Overall, the average temperature remains relatively consistent, fluctuating between 26°C and 30°C, reflecting Malaysia’s stable tropical climate. A slight increase in temperature is observed from January to April, followed by a modest decline during the mid-year months (May to September). The red dashed line marks the Southwest Monsoon Transition, which typically brings warmer and drier conditions from March onward. Despite minor regional variations—such as slightly higher temperatures in inland states like Kedah and Kuala Lumpur compared to coastal or highland regions—the overall trend indicates minimal temperature variation nationwide. The dropdown menu labeled “Select a State” allows users to focus on specific states for closer examination.

Humidity by State

The chart beside illustrates the average humidity levels in 2022 by state, displayed in a circular (polar) format representing the months of the year. Each bar radiating from the center corresponds to a day within the year, with color intensity indicating the average humidity percentage — ranging from around 70% (purple tones) to over 90% (yellow tones). The chart enables a clear visualization of seasonal humidity trends, showing that humidity generally remains high throughout the year, typical of Malaysia’s tropical climate. Some months, particularly April to June, show slightly lower humidity levels (more purple and green shades), while December to January tend to have higher humidity (more yellow shades), coinciding with the monsoon season.
